## Key findings first: Europe's geothermal position at a glance (2022–2025)

Across the 2022–2025 publication window, European geothermal electricity sits in the low single-digit gigawatts. The most recent statistical release puts capacity at 4.0 GW at end-2024, growing 4% year on year, and situates geothermal at under 1% of the region's renewable electricity capacity — a small but expanding niche [Global Renewables Statistics Agency — Renewable Capacity Statistics 2025, 2025-03-27]. The earlier market report recorded 3.5 GW at end-2022 across 142 operating plants, with growth concentrated in Türkiye, Italy and Iceland [European Geothermal Council — Geothermal Market Report 2022, 2023-02-14].

Heating is the larger and faster-moving side of the sector in project-count terms, and the policy discussion in the retrieved evidence converges on two levers: permitting speed and geological risk cover.

## Policy direction: permitting as the binding constraint

The clearest policy signal in the retrieved evidence is that administrative timelines, not geology, gate deployment. A twelve-country European review found the median time from exploration licence to plant commissioning was 6.5 years, and identified permitting rather than resource risk as the binding constraint on geothermal policy [Permitting bottlenecks for geothermal power in Europe: a 12-country review, 2024-06-01].

Industry perception matches the academic finding: survey respondents rank permitting time as the top market barrier, ahead of geological risk [European Geothermal Council — Geothermal Heating and Cooling Outlook 2024, 2024-06-20]. This alignment between measured lead times and perceived barriers is the strongest supported claim about policy direction in this evidence set.

## Policy direction: geological risk insurance and access to capital

Geological risk ranks second and access to capital third among market barriers reported by survey respondents, and only six countries operate a public risk-insurance scheme [European Geothermal Council — Geothermal Heating and Cooling Outlook 2024, 2024-06-20]. Where such schemes were introduced, the effect on market structure is measurable: in four European countries, public geological risk insurance raised the share of exploratory wells drilled by non-utility developers from 12% to 31% within five years [Geological risk insurance schemes as geothermal policy instruments in Europe, 2022-11-30]. The implication in the sources is that risk cover broadens the developer base beyond incumbent utilities, though the schemes remain limited in number.
