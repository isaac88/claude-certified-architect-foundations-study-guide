# 5.6 Information provenance

## Structured claim–source mappings

Every finding carries:

- Claim  
- Source URL  
- Document name  
- Relevant excerpt  
- Publication (or collection) date  

Downstream agents **preserve and merge** these mappings through synthesis. If you flatten to prose, **attribution dies** (1.3 practice scenario). Isolation means if synthesis never received the mapping, citations cannot appear.

## Conflicts

Two credible sources, two statistics. **Do not pick one. Do not average.**

Annotate **both values with sources**. Let the consumer (human, or a later policy) decide. Different **dates** often explain the gap — that is why dates are required, not optional colour.

## Temporal awareness

Require publication / data-collection dates in the structured output. 2019 capacity vs 2024 capacity is not a contradiction.

## Content-appropriate rendering

| Content | Render |
|---|---|
| Financial figures | Tables |
| News / narrative | Prose |
| Technical findings | Structured lists |

Do not squash everything into one uniform paragraph. Tables keep numbers checkable; prose hides them (5.1 again).

## Practice

Synthesis drops URLs; search and document spokes logged them. Two geothermal capacity figures disagree.

**Fix:** structured mappings through the coordinator prompt; synthesis emits both rows with dates; table for the numbers.

## Check questions

1. Why is averaging two capacity figures worse than showing both?
2. What dies if you summarise spoke output into a paragraph before synthesis?
