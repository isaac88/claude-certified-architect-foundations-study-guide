# 5.2 Escalation and ambiguity

## Three valid triggers

1. **Customer explicitly asks for a human** — honour **immediately**. Do **not** investigate first.
2. **Policy exception or gap** — request sits outside documented policy (competitor price-match when policy only covers own-site promotions).
3. **Cannot make meaningful progress** — capability limit, same blocker after genuine attempts.

## Two unreliable triggers (exam bait)

- **Sentiment / frustration score** — anger does not mean the case is complex. Password reset + shouting is still a password reset.
- **Self-reported confidence** — models are often **over-confident on hard** cases and **nervous on easy** ones. Same trap as using confidence instead of criteria in 4.1.

Calibrate support with **explicit criteria + few-shot** of resolve vs escalate (Domain 4.2), not a 70% slider.

## Frustration nuance

| Situation | Do |
|---|---|
| Straightforward issue, customer is frustrated, has **not** asked for a human | Acknowledge frustration, **offer the resolution** |
| They **reiterate** they want a human after you offered help | Then escalate |
| First message: “I want a human” | Escalate **now**. No “let me just look that up” |

## Ambiguous identity

Lookup returns multiple customers. **Ask for another identifier** (email, phone, order number).  

Do **not** pick most recent, most active, or first row. Heuristic selection is how refunds hit the wrong account (then 1.4 still matters, but 5.2 is the matching rule).

## Practice

Agent escalates every negative-sentiment chat, including “password reset I’m annoyed”. Complex policy exceptions stay in-bot because confidence is 82%.

**Fix:** explicit triggers above; few-shot; never sentiment/confidence as policy.

Five “Ada Lovelace” rows. Agent picks the most recently active.

**Fix:** ambiguity error + ask for email/order id.

## Check questions

1. Customer: “Get me a person.” Ticket looks like a tracking lookup. First action?
2. Why is “escalate if confidence < 70%” the wrong architecture?

## Connects to 5.3

When you do **not** escalate, failures still need to travel as structured errors — not empty success.
