# 5.5 Human review and confidence calibration

## Aggregate metrics trap

**97% overall** can hide **40% error on one document type** (handwritten invoices, a new supplier template). Do not automate “high confidence” until you slice by **document type and field**.

## Stratified random sampling

Ongoing: sample **high-confidence** extractions (and each stratum). That is how you catch **novel** error patterns that never appeared in the original test set.

Random 1% of everything undersamples the rare, ugly type.

## Field-level confidence

- Model outputs **confidence per field**
- **Calibrate** thresholds on a **labelled** validation set (ground truth)
- Route **low-confidence fields** to humans
- Spend scarce reviewers on **highest uncertainty** (and high impact: amounts, account numbers)

Uncalibrated self-reported 0.92 is not a policy (5.2). After calibration it is a **routing** signal (4.6 overlap).

## Practice

Leadership: “97% accurate — turn off human review.” Handwritten invoices are 8% of volume and 40% error.

**Answer:** stratify; keep humans on that type and on low-confidence fields; do not use the headline number.

## Check questions

1. Why sample **high-confidence** items, not only the ones the model already flagged as unsure?
2. What must exist before a confidence threshold is meaningful?

## Connects to 5.6

Reviewers (and downstream agents) cannot adjudicate without **sources**. Provenance is how conflicts stay honest.
