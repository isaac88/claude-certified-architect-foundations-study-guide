# Interdisciplinary Research Group — Annual Report 2026

This report summarises the year's work across our medical research, software
engineering, and finance teams. Each section was contributed by its own
department head. Figures cover January through December 2026.

## Medical Research Findings

The vector-borne disease team completed its three-year field study on tick and
insect populations in the northern test region. Reported bug bites among study
participants fell by 34% following the habitat-management programme. Lyme
disease transmission, which is carried by tick bites, showed a corresponding
decline across all monitored counties. The team published four peer-reviewed
papers this year. Laboratory analysis confirmed that early antibiotic
intervention remains effective in 96% of promptly reported cases. Funding for
the next phase has been approved by the national health council. Recruitment
for the expanded cohort begins in March.

## Software Engineering Updates

The platform team shipped eleven releases this year across the data pipeline
and the public API. Engineers fixed 128 bugs in total, of which 17 were
classified as critical. Mean time to resolution for critical defects dropped
from nine days to two. The migration from the legacy queue to the new event
bus completed in August with zero data loss. Test coverage rose from 71% to
88%, and the team adopted mandatory code review for every change touching
payment flows. Three engineers joined the team in the autumn hiring round.

## Financial Overview

Total operating expenditure came to 4.2 million, three percent under budget.
Research grants accounted for 61% of income, up from 55% last year. The
software division's infrastructure costs fell by 18% after the cloud
consolidation project. The finance committee approved a reserve fund equal to
six months of operating costs. External audit concluded in October with no
material findings.

## Future Outlook

Next year the medical team expands its study to two additional regions, while
the engineering group focuses on the real-time analytics platform. A joint
data-science working group will be formed in the second quarter. The board
expects headcount to grow by roughly ten percent. Continued investment in
automated testing and field monitoring equipment remains the top budget
priority for both departments.
